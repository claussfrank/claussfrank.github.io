The diagnosis nobody had run: why Tienda Novartis never told customers when their invoice would arrive

By mapping the purchase flow myself, I found that at no point in the process was the customer told when they would receive their invoice — and I designed the communication fix together with my manager.

Domain
Digital health / E-commerce
Duration
Rapid diagnosis (one meeting + 1 day of mapping)
Team
My own diagnosis + external agency
Role
Diagnosis and communication decision

An omission nobody had spotted

Many of the medications sold in Tienda Novartis are expensive and are covered by insurers that require the invoice for reimbursement — this is not a minor administrative detail. In a meeting I noticed that at no point in the process, neither on the site nor in any email, was the customer told when the invoice would be issued.

The next day I mapped the entire purchase flow myself and confirmed it. I asked the agency managing the site directly: the invoice could only be issued once the customer had the product in hand (24 hours after delivery, due to the returns policy) — but that reason never reached the customer.

Communicating what could not be changed

Issuing the invoice depended on a real technical process that could not be modified. The solution had to be about communication, not about the process itself.

The decision

Together with my manager, we decided to add an explanation of the invoicing cycle at two touchpoints: the order confirmation email and the site's Help section.

The trade-off

I chose a communication fix — faster to implement — over a process fix, which would have meant changing the technical logic of invoicing, beyond my scope and beyond the time I had while juggling multiple management tasks in parallel.

Outcome

The communication fix was implemented. I did not quantify call volume before or after the change — there was no formal follow-up, partly because the project later passed entirely into the hands of an external agency.

Months later, a formal diagnosis run by that agency independently confirmed several of the same findings I had already flagged (including responsiveness and button contrast issues, outside the scope of this case). I mention it as external validation of my judgment, without claiming credit for the agency's later work — I did not carry this project end to end.

What I learned

Mapping the flow myself, instead of relying only on what the Call Center reported secondhand, is what let me find the real root cause. It is the same pattern I repeated later on the Money Transfer project, when I went to collect my own transfer as a customer: sometimes the best research is living the process yourself.
