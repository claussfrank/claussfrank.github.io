About me

Hi, I'm Clau: I research and analyze behavior to ground decisions, and that helps me build functional interfaces that solve a real need, drawing on graphic design as a visual language to communicate a brand clearly and consistently. I look for my proposals to connect development engineering, user needs, and business goals.

So this doesn't feel tedious, think of my portfolio as a trip out to the countryside, the woods, or anywhere outside the city: out there, it's just you and the gear you brought with you. Every project, then, is a different expedition. Some are already built and need improvements; others start from scratch. In some I can propose and implement changes; in others, technical and business constraints set how far I can go. What never changes is the goal: that every decision has a reason behind it, no matter how much the terrain shifts. When design, business, and development align, it's not just that a need gets solved the user comes back to use the product without a second thought.

That's why I invite you to keep reading and see how this turns into a product or a new feature. But if you're short on time, go straight to projects you can come back to this later.

Soft Skills

No matter the destination, there's gear that can't be missing from the pack. I won't say I've mastered all of it I'm still working on my own growth but these are the pieces that can't be missing from any project or adventure. That's why my fellow travelers say it's easy to walk alongside me. I've gotten more than a few comments to that effect.

Teamwork:

Out in the woods, or anywhere far from the city, keeping your feet warm and comfortable is crucial for staying on the trail knowing how to work as a team is just as crucial. It's built on respect, real listening, and active collaboration. My teammates are those boots: they hold my decisions steady, and I lean on them to make every project better.

Strategic thinking:

A map gives you the full view of the terrain. That's what strategic thinking does for a project: see everything on the table and chart the shortest route to the goal, without losing sight of the challenges along the way.

Persuasion:

The headlamp is the information I gather from studies or tests I've run: I use it to light up my decisions and explain why a prototype turned out the way it did when I present it. For example, I sometimes need to give a developer context on a flow adjustment, showing them what I based the change on that helps them empathize, and even if it takes them more time, it'll change the experience for someone they'll never meet.

Resilience:

It's the rope that holds you when you slip. No matter how many times you stumble, what matters is moving forward because in any expedition or project, the one constant is change: the terrain shifts, the plan shifts, and not always in the direction you expected. Part of this is also learning which battles are worth letting go of, and which ones are worth holding onto.

Empathy:

It's my compass. It doesn't just point toward the user it points toward my own team too. If I'm about to ask for a big change, I think about how it affects their work, and what I can give them to make it easier: information in a format they'll actually use, direct links to the changes, colors translated into the language of the system they're building in. I empathize with the Product Owner: I give them, for example, a report or evidence of the benefits the change brings, so they can make the case to the business. And, above all, with the user: understanding the context they're using what I designed in, how stressed they are, what's going on around them in that moment.
