From a confusing flow to a clear process: redesigning Money Transfer Withdrawal in XPOS

I redesigned the Money Transfer Withdrawal flow inside OXXO's XPOS system — a mixed .NET + Delphi/VCL architecture — after finding, through the PO's reports and my own field research as a customer, that cashiers were mistaking action messages for error messages and abandoning the transaction when faced with a long, unclear form.

Domain
Retail / Transactional (POS)
Duration
6 months
Team
UX/UI Designer (solo)
Role
End-to-end UX/UI design

The symptom and the root cause

The service was being started but abandoned halfway through. The design team had no direct channel to the cashiers — everything came through the PO, who reported that action messages were being read as error messages.

To see it for myself, I went to an OXXO store as a customer and tried to request the service on three separate occasions. I confirmed what the PO had reported: the form was far too long, it was confusing, and the next steps were not clear either to the cashier or to me as the customer.

Designing inside a system that could not be broken

Before proposing any visual change, I mapped the system's full modular architecture (XPOS in .NET plus transactional services in Delphi/VCL). The VCL engine does not support HEX colors, and no change could interrupt transactional continuity in production.

What was ruled out

I presented a proposal with an extra step in the flow, better distribution and more clarity — but it meant a harder technical challenge. The team prioritized technical feasibility over the clearest possible solution.

The trade-off

The system could not reach the visual quality of a modern stack. I designed the closest version possible within that limitation, trading visual polish for guaranteed compatibility.

Outcome

The project was delivered with formal documentation, and with Nielsen's heuristics and Gestalt principles applied rigorously. I left the project while it was in testing, before results were measured in production.

This case has no impact figure to show, and I would rather say so plainly than inflate it: it stands on the rigor of the diagnosis and of the design decision under real technical constraints, not on a number.

What I learned

When your only feedback channel is an intermediary, there are things you will not see until you live them yourself. That was the reason behind going to collect my own money transfer as a customer — and it also let me spot additional opportunities that fell outside the scope of this project.
