Grupo Ti México · Client: OXXO

39 usability tests to settle a single button: the new Look & Feel for Autocobro

I led the new Look & Feel for OXXO's Autocobro self-checkout system — UI Kit, illustrations, visual and audio alerts, and simultaneous design for two screen formats (portrait and landscape). Together with my teammate Sofi, we ran 39 remote usability tests in Maze to validate which of 3 screen variants communicated the next action most clearly.

Domain
Retail / Self-service (self-checkout)
Duration
12 months (MVP4 + MVP4.1)
Team
UX/UI Designer + joint research
Role
Led the Look & Feel and executed the UI

Long queues caused by a screen nobody understood

When the "Purchase summary" screen appeared, users tried to dismiss it by tapping anywhere on the display; when it would not go away, some tried scanning another product and others simply switched queues. The screen did not communicate that it was informational, not interactive.

With 39 remote tests across 3 design variants we confirmed why: users read the amount due as an interactive button, the red boxes as active buttons (false affordance), and the splash screen copy as something actionable.

Designing fast, for two screens, without touching the flow

The project was strictly a visual change — the logic of the existing flows could not be modified. On top of that, it had to work across two screen formats in parallel (portrait and landscape, on different hardware resolutions), and switching orientation was not handled by a button in the interface but by a system-level flag, outside the control of design.

What was ruled out

We tested and discarded 2 variants: Option 1 performed worst (taps outside the screen, slowest reaction time). Option 2 improved focus but still produced accidental taps on the close button.

The trade-off

We only had 2 days for testing and presentation. We moved forward with the fastest option and the best reaction time, even though not even that one achieved full comprehension: 10 of 14 users (71%) understood the button correctly.

Outcome

The winning option was the only one with 0 taps outside the modal and the fastest reaction time. The system went to production in August 2025 — unlike other projects, this one was fully implemented — and I closed the process with a training video so corporate staff could get familiar with the changes.

There was no formal follow-up on a business KPI after launch — the project closed a few weeks after testing. This case stands on the rigor of the research under time pressure, not on a production impact figure.

What I learned

When time is this short, test instructions have to be explicit — a bias there is expensive in the results. And the whole team needs to be aligned on the objective. The training video was a natural extension of that same idea: a redesign is not finished until the people operating it understand it as well as the person who designed it.
